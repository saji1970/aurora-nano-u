01 · The concept

A phone's worth of computer,
folded into a pair of frames.

Most display glasses are accessories that lean on a phone. Aurora is built to stand on its own: it runs Android apps itself, shows them on both lenses, and connects to the internet without a phone nearby. The design keeps the weight and the heat split across both arms so it still wears like glasses.

See it

A tiny full-colour microLED projector in each end piece shines into a waveguide lens that carries the image to your eye.

Run it

An XR2 Gen 2-class processor with 16 GB of memory and 256 GB of storage runs Android apps on the glasses themselves.

Connect it

5G sub-6 GHz, Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 5.4, with antennas hidden behind plastic windows in the arms.

Think with it

On-device AI on the processor's own engine today, with a board footprint kept free for the Aurora-Nano language-model chip later.

Frame styles

Nine shapes, same insides.

The electronics live in the arms and the hinge blocks, so the front can take whatever shape people already like to wear: acetate, metal wire or a mix of both, with glass lenses that take a prescription, a sun tint or a clear anti-reflection coat. The projector at each hinge and the waveguide in each lens stay the same across every style.

03 · On model

How it sits on a face.

The arms rest on the ears like ordinary glasses. The batteries ride behind the ears to balance the weight of the lenses at the front.

These are 3D renders on sculpted display busts, built from the same model as the product renders. They stand in for photography of real people wearing prototypes, which can replace them once proof-of-concept frames exist.

04 · Inside

Every part has a place.

The frame splits the electronics between the two arms: the main computer on the right, the radios on the left, a battery behind each ear and a projector at each hinge.

05 · Design

From block diagram to frame.

The design started as a list of what the glasses must do, became an electrical block diagram, then a packaging plan that fits each board into a 6.5 mm thick arm.

Exploded view

Each part pulled out along the direction it is assembled. Two rigid boards, joined by flexible circuits that loop around the hinge pins so the arms fold without creasing them.

  • Right arm: 60 × 13 mm main board with the processor, 16 GB memory stacked on top, storage and power management
  • Left arm: radio board with the 5G modem, Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth, charger and audio
  • Behind each ear: a 46 × 10 × 6 mm cell, about 1.3 Wh each
  • At each hinge: a 6 × 6 × 10 mm microLED projector

Main-board block diagram

The application processor sits at the centre with memory, storage, the display bridge to both projectors, cameras, touch, audio and the radios around it. Power comes from a dedicated PMIC fed by the two arm batteries.

The biggest engineering risk is power: the full feature set can draw 4–10 W against a 2.7 Wh battery and a frame that can only shed 1–2 W of heat. The plan measures it on a proof of concept before any custom board is made.

Packaging plan

Where every board, cell and flex goes, with the clearances that set the frame's size. About 1 W of heat goes into each arm, spread by a graphite sheet to the outer shell and kept off the skin by an air gap.

06 · Roadmap

Concept to first 5,000 units
in about 30 months.

Each stage retires one big risk before the next stage is funded.

Done

Concept and design

Requirements, main-board block diagram, packaging plan, concept renders and a cost and sourcing estimate.

Months 0–6

Proof of concept

Development kit plus microLED and waveguide evaluation kits on a 3D-printed frame. Proves display, apps, battery life and heat.

Months 6–15

Engineering prototypes

Custom boards and frame in two EVT builds of about 50 units, with pilot users and pre-compliance radio tests.

Months 15–30

Certify and ship

DVT and PVT builds, tooling, FCC, CE and carrier certification, then the first 5,000 units.

Battery life and heat

Measured on the proof of concept before any custom board; AR1+ and dropping always-on 5G are ready fallbacks.

Weight and comfort

Weight target set at pre-seed; 8 GB memory, a single-eye display or no 5G each save weight.

Qualcomm access

Work through an ODM or design house that already holds access; a pre-seed milestone.

Display supply and cost

Evaluate two waveguide suppliers early and keep a single-eye or monochrome option.

Certification

Pre-compliance scans in the seed stage; 5G carrier approval can follow launch.

Competition

Differentiate on standalone apps and on-device AI; start with a niche such as business or accessibility users.
